# Eliminate the tie, model the slip softly

FemoraX should couple embedded piles and beams to the soil by **exact kinematic elimination**: each beam node's translations become the trilinear interpolation of its host hexahedron, `u_b = sum N_a(xi_b) u_a`. It should enforce rigid links, rigid diaphragms and equalDOF ties by the same elimination, with each rigid group's small mass block inverted exactly. Pile-soil slip, gap and friction should then be added as *physically soft* interface laws, never as stiff tie penalties. This is how production explicit codes treat kinematic constraints. Abaqus constrains embedded nodes "to the interpolated values" of the host ([Abaqus 2025](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAECSTRefMap/simacst-c-embeddedelement.htm)), and its rigid bodies "do not affect the global time increment" ([Abaqus/Explicit](https://abaqus-docs.mit.edu/2017/English/SIMACAEITNRefMap/simaitn-c-expcontacttrouble.htm)). It also fits FemoraX's existing machinery: DOF elimination, per-element equation maps, the deterministic gather and the element eigenvalue bound. Today's OpenSees route, the penalty ASDEmbeddedNodeElement, ties a node to a sub-tetrahedron of the hex with a default penalty of 1e18 scaled by the host size. That stiffness is the reason it sets the explicit step, and its tet interpolation does not even match the hex field the soil uses. Mortar and Lagrange-multiplier formulations (the Popp/Meier line of work) are the most rigorous, but they produce saddle-point systems that central difference cannot use, so they are a source of lessons on integration and locking rather than a first implementation. Reading the OpenSees source turned up two findings to record as deviations. First, OpenSees' explicit diagonal system drops the translation-rotation coupling of a transformed rigid-diaphragm mass, which is exact only when the master node sits at the floor's centre of mass. Second, ZeroLengthContactASDimplex has a shadowed-variable bug that zeroes its tangential tangent during plastic sliding. The main open risks are these: no published validation of any embedded-beam formulation in explicit seismic analysis, a narrow mesh-size window outside which line-embedded beams are mesh dependent or locked, and no theory yet for local time stepping with block masses or nonlinear interfaces across levels.

## Penalty ties set the step; elimination costs nothing

Four families of embedded-beam formulation exist. They differ most in how they enforce the tie, and the enforcement is what decides both the explicit stable step and how well the method fits a matrix-free GPU code.

**Kinematic elimination** is the Abaqus `*EMBEDDED ELEMENT` model. Embedded nodes "have their common translational DOFs constrained to the interpolated values" of the host, with weights set by the node's position. Nodes on a face or edge tie only to that face or edge. The embedded elements' rotations "are not constrained by the embedding". Roundoff weights are cleaned once at the start, which "does not create any strain". The embedded elements' mass and stiffness are added on top of the host's, so the volumes overlap ([Abaqus 2025 docs](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAECSTRefMap/simacst-c-embeddedelement.htm)). In explicit codes the general rule for kinematic constraints is that they add no stiffness. Abaqus/Explicit applies its constraint forces as "nodal acceleration corrections" ([Abaqus kinematic constraints overview](https://ceae-server.colorado.edu/v2016/books/usb/pt08ch35s01abo32.html)). It also notes that constraints "have the effect of compressing the eigenvalue spectrum", which makes the element-by-element stable-step estimate conservative ([Abaqus 2025, Explicit Dynamic Analysis](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-expdynamic.htm)).

**Penalty ties** are OpenSees' ASDEmbeddedNodeElement (Petracca, ASDEA) and the penalty forms of LS-DYNA and OpenSees' EmbeddedBeamInterfaceP. The OpenSees source (commit `955ceb0a5`, `SRC/element/CEqElement/ASDEmbeddedNodeElement.cpp`) shows the following:

- The retained support is a 3-node triangle or a 4-node tetrahedron only, never a hexahedron.
- The constraint rows are `B = [-I | N1 I | ... | N4 I]` and the stiffness is `K = iK BᵀB`.
- The default `K` is **1.0e18**. It is scaled by `sqrt(V)` in the 2D (triangle) variants and by `cbrt(V)` in the 3D tetrahedral ones (lines 849-1429).
- The element is linear, with `F = K U` on total displacements, zero mass and no state.
- The optional `-rot` tie adds `curl(u)/2` of the host.

Femora tetrahedralizes the assembled hex mesh, locates the containing tet of each beam point, and writes one element per point. It uses a penalty of `1e10` by default, and `EmbeddedBeamSolidInterface` uses `1e12` (`../Femora/src/femora/components/interface/embedded_node_interface.py`, `embedded_beam_solid_interface.py`).

The step penalty follows directly. A spring of stiffness k between lumped masses m₁ and m₂ adds a mode with ω² ≈ k(1/m₁ + 1/m₂), and dt ≤ 2/ω (our analysis, standard explicit stability). Penalties of 1e10 to 1e18 put that mode orders of magnitude above the soil's highest frequency. That matches the user's experience that the tie sets the step. A second, quieter defect is that the tie uses the linear interpolation of a sub-tetrahedron (4 of the hex's 8 corners). The beam therefore follows a slightly different soil field from the one the soil elements themselves use (our analysis of the source).

**Material-based penalties** pick the penalty from the soil rather than as a large number. LS-DYNA's `*CONSTRAINED_BEAM_IN_SOLID` penalty springs take their stiffness from the bulk moduli of beam and solid, scaled by PSSF. This is the variant recommended for impact and blast, and it conserves kinetic energy ([LSTC CBIS](https://ftp.lstc.com/anonymous/outgoing/hao/cbis)). In FLAC3D, the explicit geotechnical code closest to FemoraX, interface stiffness is held to about **10× the stiffest neighbouring zone's** `(K + 4G/3)/Δz_min`, and Itasca advises "the lowest stiffness consistent with small interface deformation" because high values slow the solution ([FLAC3D choice of properties](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/flac3d/docproject/source/theory/interfaces/choiceofproperties.html)). LS-DYNA's SOFT=1 contact goes one step further and ties the penalty to the nodal mass and the time step, k ∝ m/Δt² ([LS-DYNA SOFT option](https://lsdyna.ansys.com/?p=13360)). Abaqus does the same for its fallback constraint penalties, which "are weighted based on the masses of nodes participating in the constraint and the stable time increment" ([Abaqus kinematic constraints](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAECSTRefMap/simacst-c-kinematic.htm)). These penalties are bounded by construction, so they slow the step only modestly, but the constraint is satisfied only approximately.

**Lagrange multipliers and mortar** come from Steinbrecher, Mayr, Grill, Kremheller, Meier and Popp. Their method defines a multiplier line load along the beam centreline, in a solid meshed without subtracting the beam ([arXiv:1912.04153](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04153); [Comput. Mech. 66, 2020](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00466-020-01907-0)). OpenSees' EmbeddedBeamInterfaceL (Ghofrani, Turello, Arduino) creates 6-DOF multiplier nodes (`SRC/element/UWelements/EmbeddedBeamInterfaceL.cpp`). Both are exact, but both give an indefinite `[K Cᵀ; C 0]` system. CG cannot solve it, and an explicit scheme would need a solve of `C M⁻¹ Cᵀ λ = r` every step. That solve is cheap only when the constraints are decoupled, which matches Abaqus' warning that implicit constraint enforcement "can degrade performance for situations with overlapping constraints" ([Abaqus 2025](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-expdynamic.htm)). The Popp group also notes that larger regularizing penalties lead to "an ill-conditioned tangential system matrix" ([arXiv:1912.04153](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04153)).

| Method | Exact tie | Explicit step | Slip / friction | Implicit operator | GPU / matrix-free fit | Production use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kinematic elimination (Abaqus embedded) | Yes (translations) | Unchanged; spectrum compressed | No (tie only); add a released direction plus a law | SPD `TᵀKT`, CG unchanged | Gather/scatter through weighted maps | Abaqus, LS-DYNA constraint form |
| Tie penalty (ASDEmbeddedNode, 1e10-1e18) | Approximate | Set by the penalty: severe | Via a nonlinear law (EP variant) | SPD but ill-conditioned | An element batch; trivial | OpenSees / Femora today |
| Material- or mass-scaled penalty / interface law | Approximate (physical compliance) | Modest, computable per element | Natural (t-z, Coulomb, gap) | SPD or non-symmetric (friction) | An element batch | FLAC3D, LS-DYNA penalty, Plaxis EB |
| Lagrange / mortar (Popp-Meier, EmbeddedBeamInterfaceL) | Yes | Needs a per-step constraint solve | With extra work | Saddle point: GMRES plus block preconditioner | Extra multiplier DOFs; global coupling | Research codes (4C/BACI), OpenSees UW elements |

## Line-embedded beams are mesh dependent outside a narrow window

All four families share a modelling limit that comes from the geometry, not from the enforcement. A beam reduced to its centreline loads the soil with a line load, and the exact 3D line-load (generalized Kelvin) solution "has a singularity ... not only in the stress field, but also in the displacement field". Refining the solid mesh therefore stops converging. In Steinbrecher et al.'s test the error stopped decreasing once solid elements fell below about 0.12 m, at roughly 0.5 % tip-displacement error against a 2D-3D reference. Their rule of thumb is **solid elements no smaller than the beam diameter** ([arXiv:1912.04153](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04153)). The geotechnical literature reports the same effect. Plaxis' original embedded beam gives load-displacement curves that are "considerably mesh-sensitive" beyond shaft capacity. Turello, Pinto and Sánchez's interaction-surface element (EB-I) was designed to overcome this ([NUMGE 2023](https://www.issmge.org/uploads/publications/51/119/NUMGE2023-109.pdf); [Turello et al. 2016, CONICET](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/38239)). Plaxis itself creates an elastic zone sized by the pile radius around the beam to avoid premature failure ([Bentley wiki](https://communities.bentley.com/products/geotech-analysis/w/wiki/56388/practice-oriented-validation-of-embedded-beam-formulations-in-geotechnical-engineering)). Even the improved formulations show **skin-traction oscillations with linear elastic soil**, and further refinement "does not necessarily converge to the exact solution" ([Granitzer & Tschuchnigg, NUMGE 2023](https://tugraz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/finite-element-formulations-for-implicit-beam-to-solid-coupling-n); [Processes 9:1739](https://mdpi.com/2227-9717/9/10/1739)).

The other side of the window is **locking**. Gauss-point-to-segment coupling over-constrains when beam elements are short relative to solid elements. This is a discrete inf-sup violation that the authors call contact locking. Mortar coupling with linear multipliers showed no locking at length ratios of 10 and 5, and segment-based integration, which splits the beam at host-element faces, passes the patch test to machine precision whatever the length ratio ([arXiv:1912.04153](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04153); [arXiv:2101.02527](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2101.02527)).

Kinematic elimination is the extreme case of point-wise coupling, so it inherits the locking risk in a sharper form. If several beam nodes fall inside one hex, the beam can deform only as that hex's trilinear field allows, which stiffens the pile in bending when the soil mesh is coarse relative to the pile's bending wavelength (our analysis). The safe working window for FemoraX's eliminated embedded beams is therefore a soil element size no smaller than the pile diameter, together with beam elements about one soil element long. Femora's mesher should report when a model leaves that window. Where the soil around a pile must be finer than the pile diameter, the right tool is perimeter coupling instead: EB-I, or OpenSees' EmbeddedBeamInterface, which places coupling points at radius r on the perimeter with kinematics `u + θ × r` (OpenSees source `955ceb0a5`, `SRC/element/UWelements/EmbeddedBeamInterfaceL.cpp`).

A smaller modelling error is the volume overlap. All of these formulations accept it. It is negligible for stiff piles in soft soil, but the soil mass inside the pile is counted twice unless the beam density is reduced by the soil density (our analysis; the Popp group states the overlap error grows with volume fraction and stiffness ratio, [arXiv:1912.04153](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04153)).

## Rotations stay with the beam; slip goes into a soft law

Neither Abaqus nor LS-DYNA ties beam rotations to the solid. A pile carries its own bending and is resisted by distributed translational coupling ([Abaqus 2025](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAECSTRefMap/simacst-c-embeddedelement.htm)). Rotational coupling does exist. OpenSees' `-rot` option ties the beam node's rotation to `curl(u)/2` of the host. That value is constant in a linear tet, but it varies through an 8-node hex and is valid only for small rotations. The objective large-rotation choice is the polar rotation of the deformation gradient, which Steinbrecher, Popp and Meier show to be L2-optimal and couple consistently with a mortar scheme ([arXiv:2107.11151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11151)). One rotation must always be dealt with: with positional coupling only, the rotation about the beam axis is unrestrained and can make the system singular ([arXiv:1912.04153](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04153)).

For FemoraX the rotation question merges with the mass standard the user has already decided. Beams compute their own rotary inertia, and explicit stages refuse massless DOFs (docs/design.md, "Structures"). With translations eliminated, the free beam rotations keep their own rotational mass. Torsion about the pile axis has the beam's polar inertia and torsional stiffness, so it is well posed without a soil tie.

Interface nonlinearity is where elimination stops and laws begin. An eliminated tie cannot slip. The industrial pattern is LS-DYNA's: CDIR releases the axial constraint, and AXFOR applies a user bond-slip law driven by the relative axial displacement of the beam node and the surrounding solid point. These options work only with the constraint form, and implicit runs also need a stiffness ([LSTC CBIS](https://ftp.lstc.com/anonymous/outgoing/hao/cbis); [LS-DYNA user group](https://groups.google.com/g/ls-dyna2/c/RZo-ko1bRGM/m/JUQo_gTYAgAJ)). In FemoraX terms, a beam node can be eliminated in its two transverse directions in a local frame while keeping a free axial DOF. That DOF is joined to the interpolated soil point by a t-z/q-z-type shaft and tip law. Shaft laws are physically soft, of order the soil's shear stiffness per tributary area, so their ω² is comparable to the soil's own and the step survives (our analysis). Lateral gapping and p-y-type behaviour need perimeter coupling, as in OpenSees' EmbeddedEPBeamInterface. That element holds an NDMaterial per interface point and drives it with the relative displacement (`EmbeddedEPBeamInterface.cpp`). Turello et al.'s elastoplastic interface for the EB-I element takes the same approach ([CONICET 11336/78731](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/78731)).

One caution belongs in Femora's documentation: **empirical p-y and t-z curves are calibrated for beam-on-springs models**. With a continuum soil around the pile they double-count the soil's compliance. The interface law in a continuum model should be a slip, gap and friction law whose elastic part is stiff relative to the soil, as in FLAC3D, not a p-y backbone (our analysis, consistent with how [FLAC3D](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/flac3d/docproject/source/theory/interfaces/formulation.html) treats interfaces).

## One prolongation handles every linear constraint

Rigid links, rigid diaphragms, equalDOF and the embedded tie are all linear constraints `u = T q`: T maps the retained DOFs q to all DOFs. The reduced dynamics are `TᵀMT q̈ + Tᵀ f_int(Tq) = Tᵀ f_ext`. This is the form matrix-free libraries already use. deal.II's `FEEvaluation` resolves constraints while it gathers and applies the transpose when it scatters, and gives constrained rows an identity block so CG sees a nonsingular operator ([deal.II step-37](https://dealii.org/current/doxygen/deal.II/step_37.html)). MFEM solves on "true" DOFs through a prolongation P, as `PᵀAP` ([arXiv:1911.09220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.09220)). Munch, Ljungkvist and Kronbichler apply hanging-node constraints on the fly on CPU and GPU and cut the extra cost of constrained cells **5-10×** on CPU ([Augsburg record](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/year/2022/docId/99791)).

FemoraX's union-find equalDOF numbering is the special case in which every row of T is a unit vector, so it costs nothing in the gather and scatter maps. Elimination keeps `TᵀKT` symmetric positive definite, so CG and Jacobi keep working. Penalty raises the condition number roughly in proportion to k_p/k, and Lagrange multipliers make the system indefinite (our analysis; the OpenSees manual quotes Cook that penalty numbers must be "large enough to be effective but not so large as to provoke numerical difficulties", [OpenSees manual](https://opensees.berkeley.edu/OpenSees/manuals/usermanual/617.htm)).

OpenSees builds its rigid constraints from initial coordinates as constant small-rotation matrices. `RigidDiaphragm` in xy with ndf = 6 constrains DOFs (0, 1, 5) with `mat(0,2) = -Δy` and `mat(1,2) = Δx`. `RigidBeam` fills the full skew matrix (`RigidDiaphragm.cpp` lines 160-185, `RigidBeam.cpp` lines 124-154). The OpenSees documentation recommends the Transformation handler for rigidDiaphragm ([OpenSees docs](https://opensees.github.io/OpenSeesDocumentation/user/manual/model/mp_constraint/rigidDiaphragm.html)). Abaqus/Explicit, by contrast, always enforces kinematic constraints geometrically nonlinearly ([Abaqus kinematic constraints](https://ceae-server.colorado.edu/v2016/books/usb/pt08ch35s01abo32.html)). Matching OpenSees means a constant T. A large-rotation T is a later, documented option.

### Explicit stages: a block mass per rigid group

Production explicit codes keep the nodal mass diagonal and integrate each rigid group as a small separate system. LS-DYNA computes 6 DOFs per rigid body, with optional user inertia tensors through `*CONSTRAINED_NODAL_RIGID_BODY_INERTIA` ([Altair CNRB](https://help.altair.com/hwsolvers/rad/topics/solvers/rad/constrained_nodal_rigid_body_lsdyna_r.htm)). It warns that an inertia tensor that is too small "could cause numerical instability in the rigid body subroutines" ([Ansys knowledge article](https://innovationspace.ansys.com/knowledge/?p=165150)).

For FemoraX the equivalent works as follows (our design analysis). A slave of mass m at offset (Δx, Δy) adds this 3×3 block to its diaphragm master's (ux, uy, θz):

```
[ m      0     -mΔy ]
[ 0      m      mΔx ]
[ -mΔy   mΔx    m(Δx²+Δy²) ]
```

A rigid link adds the corresponding 6×6 block. Because T is constant, each group's block is assembled and inverted once per stage. The master's acceleration is that inverse times the gathered group force, and every other DOF keeps its diagonal. The cheapest GPU form leaves the element kernels untouched. One small kernel expands master values to slave nodes (`u_s = T u_m`) before the force pass, and another contracts slave forces onto the masters (`f_m += Tᵀ f_s`) after it, in a fixed order. The extra traffic is proportional to the number of constrained nodes. Folding weights into the element maps instead, as deal.II does, gives variable-length maps and warp divergence.

The embedded tie fits the same machinery. Its weights are the hex shape functions, and row-sum lumping of the beam mass (`m_a += N_a m_b`, with N_a ≥ 0 inside the hex) keeps the mass diagonal and conserves total mass and translational momentum. This is not confirmed as Abaqus/Explicit's choice. Elements whose nodes all lie in one rigid group have no deformation and drop out of the stable-step estimate. For the rest, the element eigenvalue bound stays valid if it uses each element's share of the reduced mass, or conservatively the unreduced nodal masses, because constraints only compress the spectrum ([Abaqus 2025](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-expdynamic.htm)).

### Implicit stages: the same T

The operator becomes `TᵀK_effT`, applied matrix-free by the same expand-apply-contract sequence, and CG is unchanged. The Jacobi diagonal at a master needs the T-weighted element diagonals plus cross terms. deal.II notes that an element-wise diagonal under constraints is only approximate ([step-37](https://dealii.org/current/doxygen/deal.II/step_37.html)). That is acceptable for a preconditioner, or the master's diagonal can be computed exactly from its small group.

## Contact and interfaces belong in element batches, bounded by mass

Interfaces in geotechnical practice come in two forms. One is volume piles with zero-thickness interfaces, the more exact option ([TU Delft thesis](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_a6c17457-9f56-4480-b8f8-1082eca22926?preview=1)). The other is embedded beams with springs, which work "particularly good" for axial pile-group serviceability ([Bentley blog](https://blog.bentley.com/?p=46763)). FLAC3D's one-sided node-to-face interface is the explicit model to emulate for volume piles and mats. It recomputes normal penetration and relative shear velocity at each interface node every step. Normal force is kn × penetration × area. The shear force is incremental and capped by cohesion plus friction on the effective normal stress, with optional bond strengths and dilation during slip ([FLAC3D interfaces](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/flac3d/docproject/source/theory/interfaces/formulation.html)). Real-ESSI uses zero-length, stress-based soft contact with hardening shear and gap viscosity in nuclear SSSI ([arXiv:2310.10251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10251)).

In explicit dynamics, kinematic predictor-corrector contact "has no influence on the stable time increment", whereas penalty contact adds stiffness. Abaqus accounts for it in its time incrementation ([Abaqus contact enforcement](https://abaqus-docs.mit.edu/2017/English/SIMACAEITNRefMap/simaitn-c-expcontactconstraints.htm)). Bi-penalty methods pair a stiffness penalty with a mass penalty in a ratio that leaves the maximum eigenfrequency, and with it the critical step, unchanged ([Kopačka et al. 2013](https://webarchiv.lib.cas.cz:443/wayback/20150411041150/http://www.it.cas.cz/files/u2130/2013_Kopacka_COMPDYN2013.pdf); [Kolman et al. 2019](https://engmechx.it.cas.cz/improc/2019/041_Kolman_082.pdf)). A 2025 study adds that large penalties "can excite high-frequency oscillations in the contact forces" ([arXiv:2511.14323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14323)).

For SSI with small sliding, FemoraX can avoid the hard part of GPU contact, which is dynamic global search. The evidence that production GPU contact is mature is thin. LS-DYNA's documented GPU work was confined to the implicit direct solver ([DYNAmore 2011](https://www.dynamore.de/de/download/papers/konferenz11/papers/session6-paper2.pdf)). Research GPU crash codes assemble contact forces with atomics and identify contact search as the dominant cost ([Zhejiang Univ. Sci. A](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1631/jzus.A2200311)). If the interface pairs are fixed at the start of each stage (zero-length, node-to-face small sliding, embedded points), each interface is just another element batch with fixed connectivity and fixed-size state. FemoraX's deterministic gather and coloring then apply unchanged. Stick, slip and open branching is a small fraction of total work, so the warp divergence it causes costs little (our analysis).

### What the OpenSees source shows about ZeroLengthContactASDimplex

This element (Akan, Petracca) is a two-node penalty Mohr-Coulomb law with IMPL-EX integration (Oliver, Huespe and Cante 2008) ([OpenSees manual](https://opensees.github.io/OpenSeesDocumentation/user/manual/model/elements/zeroLengthContactASDimplex.html)). Reading `SRC/element/zeroLength/ZeroLengthContactASDimplex.cpp` at `955ceb0a5` gives the following.

`getMass()` returns zero, so the element adds stiffness but no mass. The normal response is compression-only, `SN = Kn·eps_n`, and the friction limit is `-mu·SN`, with no cohesion, tension strength or dilation. `time_factor` is forced to 1, by design, because of load-control pseudo-time.

In the implicit plastic tangent (lines 1077-1086), `double dE1` and `double dE2` are redeclared inside the `if (UTsqnorm > eps)` block. The outer zeros therefore survive, `dDdE1 = dDdE2 = 0`, and the tangential columns `C(1,1)`, `C(2,1)`, `C(1,2)`, `C(2,2)` are assigned zero. The assignment uses `=`, so it also overwrites the secant `(1 - damage)·Kfriction` set just above. Even with the shadowing fixed, that `=` needs checking against `+=`. The residual is unaffected, so the bug costs Newton iterations, not accuracy.

`recvSelf` reads `gap0(1) = ddata(39)`, but `sendSelf` writes `ddata(29)` (lines 542 and 613), which affects only restart and parallel transfer.

None of these has been confirmed by a test or reported upstream. IMPL-EX itself is irrelevant to central difference, which already evaluates every law explicitly.

## Local time stepping and multi-GPU follow the "one group, one level" rule

No published analysis covers stabilized leapfrog LTS (Diaz-Grote with the Grote-Michel-Sauter stabilization, [arXiv:2005.13350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13350)) across rigid bodies, general MPCs or nonlinear interfaces. The subcycling literature reports lost momentum conservation and stability problems at level interfaces ([Karimi & Nakshatrala, arXiv:1305.6355](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.6355)), and "challenges still remain with respect to stability and accuracy at interfaces" ([Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:fa716278-95b0-4048-aa60-27e11c41b78a/files/rs1784n240)). The nearest production precedent is SeisSol's clustered LTS, which runs nonlinear dynamic-rupture fault interfaces and gained 6.8× over global stepping on CPU ([EGU2018-15363](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU2018/EGU2018-15363.pdf?pdf)). SeisSol is a DG code, however, and its flux coupling does not transfer directly. Asynchronous variational integrators show that penalty layers can each run at their own rate and still conserve momentum and energy ([arXiv:1007.3240](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.3240)).

The consequences for FemoraX follow from this (our analysis). Elimination merges a constraint group into retained equations, so each rigid body, diaphragm or embedded pile sits in one level by construction. That level is the finest of any element touching it. A diaphragm on a fine-level floor pulls every beam and column of that floor into the fine level, which repeats at structural scale the laminar-boundary finding already recorded in docs/design.md.

An eliminated embedded pile is different from a penalty-tied one. Its beam elements act directly on soil-hex equations, and their own `critical_dt`, set by axial EA/L and the beam's rotary inertia, places the pile and its ring of hexes in a level. A penalty tie instead sets a level of its own through ω² ≈ k_p/m, almost always the finest. This is one more argument for elimination.

The energy argument behind the LTS scheme needs M symmetric positive definite and A symmetric, and both survive `Tᵀ(·)T`. A block-diagonal M with non-overlapping group blocks inverts exactly, level by level. The derivation still has to be checked rather than assumed. Nonlinear interfaces (stick-slip, gap) should keep both sides in one level, verified with long runs and an energy balance that includes friction work.

Across GPUs, the design's rule that the owner of the retained node resolves a tie extends to embedded beams. The beam's nodes belong to the rank that owns the host hex, and a pile crossing a partition is just elements with ghost equations. A diaphragm spanning ranks needs one 3- or 6-vector reduction per group per step. Alternatively, Femora/METIS can weight the partition so that each floor stays whole. Interface elements are few and cheap, and they belong in the partition weights that LTS will already introduce.

## Recommendation for FemoraX

**Implement exact kinematic elimination first**, as a general weighted constraint `u = T q`. It covers rigid links, rigid diaphragms, pile-cap links and the tied embedded beam, whose weights are the 8-node hex shape functions evaluated at each beam node. Implement it as expand and contract kernels around unchanged element kernels. In explicit stages use row-sum-lumped embedded masses and an exactly inverted 3×3 (diaphragm) or 6×6 (rigid link or body) block per group. In implicit stages use `TᵀK_effT` with CG.

Read Femora's ASDEmbeddedNodeElement as "tied" and give it elimination semantics. This is a compatibility-level-2 deviation: elimination is the limit of the penalty as K → ∞, and it uses the hex's own interpolation rather than a sub-tetrahedron's. Verify it against OpenSees at Femora's penalty on small models, and expect agreement to a relative error of order k_soil/K. Map `-rot` to the hex's `curl(u)/2` at the point as an opt-in, documented as small-rotation and prone to locking. By default, leave beam rotations free with their own rotary inertia, as Abaqus and LS-DYNA do. Refuse overconstraints such as an embedded node also tied by a rigid link, as Abaqus does ([Abaqus 2025](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAECSTRefMap/simacst-c-embeddedelement.htm)), unless the composition is defined explicitly. Report beams that fall outside the window of solid elements at least the pile diameter in size and beam elements about one soil element long. Offer subtraction of the overlapped soil mass as an option.

**Then add soft interface laws, never tie penalties.** The second step is the LS-DYNA AXFOR pattern: transverse elimination in a local frame, a free axial DOF and a t-z/q-z law with fixed-size state. The third is a perimeter-point interface in the EB-I / EmbeddedEPBeamInterface style, with an NDMaterial-like slip-gap-friction law for lateral gapping, as an element batch. Every interface element declares `max_omega_sq` from its stiffness and its share of the nodes' masses, so the stable-step check and the LTS levels see it. Stiffnesses default to physical values or to FLAC3D's ≤ 10× zone stiffness. An optional mass-scaled bound (k ≤ c·m/Δt², SOFT=1 style) is reported whenever it is active. Port ZeroLengthContactASDimplex for compatibility, with the Backward-Euler law exact, a consistent tangent and a mass-aware step. Mortar and Lagrange coupling stay for later, implicit only, and only if a validation case shows that perimeter penalties are not enough.

**Record as deviations** in docs/deviations.md:

- **Rigid diaphragms in explicit stages.** OpenSees' `DiagonalSOE::addA` keeps only `m(i,i)` of the transformed mass, which drops `−mΔy` and `mΔx`. With `-lumped` it adds column sums that mix mass and mass·length units (`DiagonalSOE.cpp` lines 179-230). Both are exact only when the master sits at the centre of mass. OpenSees' implicit Transformation path assembles `TᵀMT` in full, so OpenSees' own explicit and implicit runs of an off-centre diaphragm disagree. FemoraX uses the exact block. A two-node OpenSees run should confirm this before the entry is final.
- **ZeroLengthContactASDimplex.** The shadowed `dE1`/`dE2`, the `=` overwrite of the secant, and the `recvSelf` index bug, each with a finite-difference tangent test as evidence.
- **The embedded tie as elimination** on the hex interpolation, as above.
- **Interface elements given a stable step.** OpenSees reports none, because it has zero mass.
- **Constant small-rotation T** kept, matching OpenSees, with large rotation a future option.

**Open risks.** No source found validates any embedded-beam formulation in explicit or seismic analysis. Every validation found is a static load test or lateral pile-group loading ([Turello et al. 2019](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019IJNAM..43..272T/abstract)). FemoraX must therefore build its own verification ladder: a patch test, an embedded elastic pile against a conforming volume-pile model, then pile-group dynamics against OpenSees. The mesh window is narrow, and the skin-traction oscillations of the published formulations apply to FemoraX too. LTS with block masses and nonlinear interfaces is research territory. Variable-length constraint maps and per-group reductions are new GPU and multi-GPU paths that need benchmarks. Exact elimination stiffens a pile embedded in a coarse mesh, and only comparison with a conforming model will show by how much.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the question. The choice is not between a penalty and a multiplier for one pile element. FemoraX needs one constraint mechanism, the prolongation T with small exact mass blocks, that serves equalDOF, rigid links, diaphragms and embedded beams alike, and leaves all stiffness and nonlinearity to element batches that know their own stable step. This splits the problem cleanly: kinematics are exact and free for the time step, and physics (slip, gap, friction) is soft and therefore cheap. It also means the explicit step should stop being set by numerical devices and be set only by real structural stiffness, which is exactly the case local time stepping is designed for.

The less comfortable implication is that the embedded-beam idea is strongest as an engineering shortcut and weakest as a converging discretization. Line coupling stops converging below the pile diameter, and point coupling locks above it. FemoraX's verification has to establish the practical window for seismic SSI, since no published study has done so, and that result would itself be a contribution.
