# Stabilized local time stepping frees the soil step

**Yes: stabilized multilevel leapfrog local time stepping (LTS) is the right core design for FemoraX, provided the structure's own discretization is fixed first.** The variant to build is the Diaz–Grote leapfrog LTS with the Chebyshev stabilization of Grote, Michel & Sauter, which has a proven stability limit that depends only on the coarse mesh. The recommended settings are a level ratio of 2, ν ≈ 0.05, all constraint groups inside one level, and the level boundary placed in elastic material. This is the only method family that is explicit, matrix-free, energy-conserving in the undamped linear case, reduces to plain central difference with one level, and has run at scale on GPUs (SPECFEM3D's Newmark-LTS; SeisSol's clustered LTS with graph-captured kernels). Its competitors each fail one FemoraX requirement. Conventional mass scaling lowers the very frequencies SSI cares about. Selective mass scaling puts a CG solve in every step. Implicit-explicit partitions need a structural linear solver at every soil step. Stabilized Chebyshev, exponential and multirate Runge–Kutta integrators either cost p operator products per step or discard leapfrog's structure. LTS is not a complete answer on its own. Published theory covers only undamped or mass-damped linear problems, so stiffness-proportional Rayleigh damping, Uniform filter damping, plasticity and penalty interfaces across levels are FemoraX's own research to derive and verify. Before any integrator work, two cheap element-level changes shrink the gap LTS has to bridge. One is a single force-based element per member, which raises a 3 m steel column's axial limit from about 1.2e-4 s to about 5.9e-4 s. The other is a deliberate lumped rotary inertia, which is needed anyway because OpenSees' beams lump none.

## Six method families, judged against one SSI workload

The workload is specific. FemoraX's soil (10^6–10^9 hexahedra) is stable at 2e-4 to 2e-3 s. Beam-columns, small bricks near foundations and penalty pile-soil interfaces drop the global step toward 1e-4 s or lower, a step ratio of roughly 2–20 for a tiny fraction of the model. Every remedy below has to keep FemoraX's one fused gather-compute-scatter kernel per batch, FP64, deterministic scatter, nonlinear history materials, Rayleigh and Uniform damping, DOF-eliminated constraints and DRM input. The table condenses how the families compare; the sections that follow give the evidence.

### Local time stepping is the explicit-explicit method proven on GPUs

Diaz & Grote's 2009 scheme splits the unknowns with a diagonal 0/1 projection P marking the locally refined region. Each coarse step Δt computes the coarse contribution `w = A(I−P)z_n` once. It then runs p leapfrog-type substeps of size Δt/p in which only `A P` is applied, and finishes with `z_{n+1} = −z_{n−1} + q_1`. With a symmetric FE discretization and (nearly) diagonal mass, it **stays explicit and exactly conserves a discrete energy** ([Diaz & Grote 2009](https://doi.org/10.1137/070709414); algorithm as presented in [Grote & Mitkova, arXiv:1205.0654](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0654)). The cost is one coarse operator product and about p fine products per coarse step. Away from the interface the coarse region is an ordinary leapfrog step. A multilevel version applies the idea recursively ([Diaz & Grote, HAL hal-01184090](https://hal-univ-pau.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01184090)), and Runge–Kutta-type multilevel LTS has accuracy and energy proofs for any number of levels ([Almquist & Mehlin, KIT CRC 1173 preprint 2016/16](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2016-16.pdf)).

The production evidence is the strongest of any family. **Rietmann, Grote, Peter & Schenk adapted multilevel Newmark-LTS to SPECFEM3D's continuous spectral elements.** It is efficient for element-size contrasts above 100x and has run on thousands of CPU cores and hundreds of GPUs ([J. Comput. Phys. 334 (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2016.11.012); [edoc.unibas.ch/59456](https://edoc.unibas.ch/59456)). The current SPECFEM3D feature table, however, lists LTS as CPU-only ([SPECFEM3D features](https://specfem3d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/)). SeisSol and EDGE run clustered rate-2 LTS for ADER-DG at petascale. EDGE reports theoretical speedups of 2.28x–2.67x over global stepping and more than 10x time-to-solution on the La Habra case at above 95 % parallel efficiency on 256–1,536 Frontera nodes ([Breuer & Heinecke, arXiv:2202.10313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10313)). The ADER-DG cluster scheme is tied to first-order DG and does not transfer to lumped-mass continuous FE. The Diaz–Grote line does: one level is plain leapfrog, which is central difference with lumped mass.

### Engineering subcycling is cheaper to write but carries instability history

Structural subcycling predates LTS and has a cautionary record. **Daniel showed that Belytschko–Lu subcycling is unstable, and that the modified Neal–Belytschko scheme is only "statistically stable"**: it fails in narrow bands of time steps below the expected limit. Only the Smolinski–Sleith algorithm was stable in the classical sense, at a cost in accuracy ([Daniel, Comput. Mech. 1998](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s004660050248)). The energy-conserving variants of Smolinski and Daniel avoid the bands. They lose accuracy when the material at the time-step interface does not deform elastically ([Gates, Matouš & Heath, IJNME 2008](https://www3.nd.edu/~kmatous/Papers/IJNME_AMVI.pdf)), which is exactly the case of plastic soil next to a pile.

Commercial codes ship subcycling but rarely recommend it. LS-DYNA's developers say it "has not been used extensively for reasons pertaining to its robustness but also due to lack of its practical needs". They report **45 % CPU saved on a RAV4 model and 9 % on an S-rail**, and pair it with mass scaling (`*CONTROL_SUBCYCLE_MASS_SCALED_PART`) ([DYNAmore 2014 LS-DYNA Forum](https://dynamore.de/de/download/papers/2014-ls-dyna-forum/documents/entwicklervortraege/current-status-of-subcycling-and-multiscale-simulations-in-ls-dyna)). Abaqus/Explicit's selective subcycling holds the interface velocity from the non-subcycling zone constant through the subcycles. It merges zones that share a constraint equation, connector, rigid body or general contact, forbids beams with exact rotary inertia on shared nodes, and warns that there is little benefit "if a majority of elements in the model are in subcycling zones" ([Abaqus 2025 Analysis Guide](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-selectivesubcycling.htm)). These restrictions predict FemoraX's own trouble spots: rigid diaphragms, rigid links, beams with rotary mass and penalty interfaces that straddle a level boundary.

### Implicit-explicit partitions pay off only at ratios in the hundreds

The Hughes–Liu element partition makes the implicit group unconditionally stable while the explicit elements' critical step governs the system ([Hughes & Liu, J. Appl. Mech. 1978](https://next.sciencegate.app/document/10.1115/1.3424304)). Its modern descendants couple subdomains with their own Newmark schemes and steps through Lagrange multipliers in dual-Schur form. The GC method enforces velocity continuity at fine steps and is **dissipative under subcycling**. Prakash–Hjelmstad enforces continuity at coarse steps only and is limited to two subdomains. Karimi & Nakshatrala's monolithic version admits any number of subdomains but does not preserve energy, shows interface drift, and finds that "subcycling need not always improve accuracy" ([Karimi & Nakshatrala, J. Comput. Phys. 2014](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1305.6355)). Mahjoubi, Gravouil & Combescure give an energy-conserving monolithic variant for large step ratios ([CMAME 2011, HAL hal-00560918](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00560918)).

The SSI results are real but come from a different regime. Brun and co-workers' implicit/explicit co-computation of the SPEAR reinforced-concrete frame saved **about 2x** ([Soil Dyn. Earthq. Eng. 2012, hal-00938628](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00938628)). Blast runs with ratios up to 1000 saved about 60 % ([record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/10/1458313.htm)). The HATI integrator couples explicit spectral-element soil (EFISPEC3D) to implicit nonlinear Code_Aster structures ([Zuchowski, Brun & De Martin 2018](https://www.em-consulte.com/it/article/1208666/co-simulation-coupling-spectralfinite-elements-for); [Brun, De Martin & Richart 2021](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/284404)). Each coarse soil step then needs a structural implicit solve, Newton on fiber beams if nonlinear, plus an interface condensation solve. FemoraX's ratio is 2–20, and its design excludes direct solvers in production. Twenty explicit substeps of a structure that is 0.1 % of the model cost less than a Newton solve of that structure. IMEX becomes the better tool only if some component needs a step hundreds of times below the soil's.

## Mass scaling is free per step but bends the frequencies SSI cares about

Conventional mass scaling (LS-DYNA `DT2MS < 0`, Abaqus fixed or variable scaling) adds diagonal mass only to elements whose step is below a target ([LS-DYNA mass scaling](https://lsdyna.ansys.com/mass-scaling/); [Abaqus docs](https://abaqus-docs.mit.edu/2017/English/SIMACAEANLRefMap/simaanl-c-massscaling.htm)). Since an element's step scales as L√(ρ/E), raising the step by a factor k needs k² more mass: 4x for a doubling, 100x for a tenfold gain. DYNAmore calls it "a broadsword" that "scales down all eigenfrequencies indiscriminately" ([DYNAmore webinar 2022](https://lsdyna.ansys.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/221215_Webinar_Mass-Scaling_and_Subcycling.pdf)). Theory confirms that each eigenfrequency of the scaled region drops by a ratio between 1 and √α, so low modes are not protected ([Voet, Sande & Buffa, arXiv:2410.23816](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23816)). The same webinar quantifies the damage on a dynamic bumper impact. **Only 0.8 % added total mass gave an 18.9 % peak contact-force error; 4.2 % gave 94 %.**

| Step (s) | Added mass | Conventional: max / mean error | Selective: max / mean error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.5e-7 | 0.013 % | 2.7 / 0.3 % | 2.7 / 0.3 % |
| 1.0e-6 | 0.8 % | 18.9 / 4.3 % | 4.7 / 0.66 % |
| 1.5e-6 | 4.2 % | 94.3 / 20 % | 10.5 / 2.7 % |
| 2.0e-6 | 11.6 % | 154 / 33.6 % | 19.7 / 5.6 % |

Source: [DYNAmore webinar 2022, slides 15 and 18](https://lsdyna.ansys.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/221215_Webinar_Mass-Scaling_and_Subcycling.pdf).

For SSI this error lands on the quantities of interest. Scaled column mass shifts structural periods and adds inertial force on the foundation. Scaled soil bricks next to a footing change kinematic and inertial interaction. Under `UniformExcitation` the load `−M·1·a_g` grows with the added mass. Mass-proportional Rayleigh damping grows too, unless FemoraX forms the αM term from a separately stored physical mass. Conventional scaling remains useful as a reported, opt-in fallback for isolated tiny elements, with the added mass and the change of the first structural periods printed per region. Vendors give no official limit for dynamic analyses; the guidance is to check added mass and energies.

**Selective mass scaling (SMS) is the scalpel.** It adds non-diagonal element mass that leaves translational rigid-body inertia unchanged and lowers only the highest frequencies, with steps raised "up to 30 times" ([Olovsson, Simonsson & Unosson, IJNME 2005](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1293)). The price is a CG solve every step. LS-DYNA measured SMS at about 54 % more cost per step than conventional scaling (997 s against 647 s for the same 15,874 steps) and recommends it only on part sets ([LS-DYNA SMS how-to](https://lsdyna.ansys.com/?p=3558)). Radioss AMS targets 10–20x steps, and the mass-matrix work can reach 50 % of total cost in highly nonlinear models ([Altair Radioss 2025](https://2025.help.altair.com/2025/hwsolvers/rad/topics/solvers/rad/increase_speed_maintain_accuracy_ams_r.htm)). Voet et al. prove that raising the step and keeping the scaled mass well conditioned "are conflicting goals". They call Olovsson's method "quite inaccurate for lower frequencies" and note that non-diagonal mass "defeats the spirit of explicit dynamics" ([arXiv:2410.23816](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23816v2)).

SMS would fit FemoraX's existing matrix-free CG if it were restricted to the equations of the step-limiting elements, but on a GPU about 20 CG iterations mean 40–60 tiny launches and reductions per step. That is the same overhead LTS faces, without LTS's physics fidelity. Every published SMS verification is crash, forming or quasi-static work, none is wave propagation or site response, and no GPU implementation was found. **Reciprocal mass matrices** build a sparse M⁻¹ directly, so the update stays one sparse product with no solve ([Tkachuk & Bischoff, IJNME 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4805); [González et al. 2018, localized Lagrange multipliers](https://core.ac.uk/download/222572567.pdf)). They are GPU-friendly, but unscaled gains are about 1.25–1.5x: a 25 % speedup with Allman shells ([Tkachuk et al., Uni Stuttgart](https://elib.uni-stuttgart.de/items/a26af4f5-c0a3-404f-adef-b6316fbb74bc/full)), and reportedly no more than about 50 % ([Voet et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23816v2)). No production code uses them, and no verified hex8-plus-beam construction with multipoint constraints exists.

## Chebyshev, exponential and multirate Runge–Kutta integrators solve a different problem

Leapfrog-Chebyshev (LFC) replaces leapfrog's `τ²L` with a stabilized degree-p Chebyshev polynomial `P_p(z) = 2 − 2T_p(ν_p − z/α_p)/T_p(ν_p)`. It is stable at a step about p times larger for p products with the stiff linear operator L. For linear problems "the p times larger CFL constant is compromised by a p times higher cost per time step" ([Carle, Hochbruck & Sturm, CRC 1173 preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)). It pays off only when L is the cheap stiff part and the nonlinearity g is the expensive part. That makes it a multirate method, and its local form is LTS. Carle & Hochbruck's error analysis covers equations where "only a few components of the linear part cause stiffness", with steps independent of the stiff part ([CRC preprint 2021/26](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2021-26.pdf)). The theory requires the stiff part to be linear and symmetric positive definite. A yielding fiber column is a nonlinear stiff part, and no damping treatment exists. LFC's real contribution to FemoraX is its stabilization idea, which Grote, Michel & Sauter carried into LTS (next section).

Exponential and Gautschi-type integrators evaluate `cos(τΩ)` and `sinc` filters of the stiff operator. With exact matrix functions they are unconditionally stable ([Hochbruck & Ostermann, Acta Numerica 2010](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492910000048)). In practice those functions come from Krylov bases that cost m operator products and O(m) global reductions per step. With plasticity the operator changes every step, and Rayleigh damping makes the first-order system non-normal. Pari, Kanungo & Gavini claim 10–100x larger steps than Newmark-β, but on small cantilever and elastomer tests against an implicit baseline at matched accuracy ([arXiv:2405.05213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05213)). Seismic exponential integrators reach only "computational efficiency similar to lower-order methods" ([Ravelo et al., arXiv:2401.16322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16322)). FemoraX's step is set by stability, not accuracy, and the problem is a few stiff elements rather than a stiff whole, so these gains do not transfer. The impulse (r-RESPA) fast/slow splitting is unstable near multiples of half the fast period, and mollification fixes it ([García-Archilla, Sanz-Serna & Skeel, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 1999](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_25821952)). This is the same resonance phenomenon that stabilized LTS removes. MRI-GARK multirate Runge–Kutta methods suit problems "where the influence of the fast component on the slow one is weak" ([Sandu, arXiv:1808.02759](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02759)). In SSI the soil drives the structure and the structure loads the soil, and an RK rewrite discards leapfrog's one-force-per-step economy and its conservation properties. Asynchronous variational integrators give every element its own step, are symplectic and designed for nonlinear elastodynamics ([Lew, Marsden, Ortiz & West 2003](https://lagrange.mechse.illinois.edu/pubs/LeMaOrWe2003/)), but their per-element event queue maps poorly onto batched GPU kernels.

## One force-based element per member closes most of the gap first

The cheapest remedy is to stop making short elements. For steel, c = √(E/ρ) ≈ 5100 m/s. The axial limit L/c of a 0.6 m displacement-based element is about **1.2e-4 s**; one 3 m element gives about **5.9e-4 s**, inside the soil's range. Bending, roughly ∝ L², gains more, provided the rotary mass is set deliberately. Force-based (flexibility) elements reproduce a member's nonlinear response with one element per member because their force interpolation satisfies equilibrium exactly. Neuenhofer & Filippou's non-iterative state determination carries the element residual into the next step ([J. Struct. Eng. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1997)123:7(958))), and Gendy & Ayoub published explicit force-based fiber elements with no element-level iterations, validated on drop-weight impact tests ([J. Struct. Eng. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)ST.1943-541X.0002061)). That fits a GPU better than OpenSees' default ForceBeamColumn3d, which iterates up to 10 times to a 1e-12 tolerance with subdivision on failure (local OpenSees clone, commit `955ceb0a5`, `SRC/element/forceBeamColumn/ForceBeamColumn3d.cpp`). The default should be a fixed-cost, branch-light kernel. Matching OpenSees bit for bit needs the iterative mode as an option and a `docs/deviations.md` entry for the default.

One element per member has its own limit: localization. With softening sections and Gauss-Lobatto integration, "the element response will change as a function of Np". Scott & Fenves' modified Gauss-Radau plastic-hinge integration restores objective response and "is thus recommended … when softening and degradation of the members is expected". No single rule represents both spread of plasticity under hardening and objective softening ([Scott & Fenves, J. Struct. Eng. 2006](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~mhscott/Scott-Fenves_JSE_2006.pdf); [Coleman & Spacone 2001](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(2001)127:11(1257))). Both rules are fixed-size, so the integration rule becomes a compile-time element parameter. For the stable step, a softening section's negative tangent does not govern; the elastic section stiffness bounds ω², the same rule FemoraX already applies to PressureDependMultiYield.

### OpenSees beams lump no rotary inertia, so FemoraX must choose one

The decisive mass finding comes from the OpenSees source (commit `955ceb0a5`). **`ForceBeamColumn3d::getMass` fills only the six translational diagonal entries with `0.5·ρ·L`; rotational mass is zero and there is no consistent option.** `DispBeamColumn3d` lumps translational mass, offers an optional consistent mass, and its torsional mass is commented out ("CURRENTLY NO TORSIONAL MASS"). `ExplicitDifference` forms its system matrix from the mass alone, so every free DOF, beam rotations included, needs nonsingular mass (`SRC/element/forceBeamColumn/ForceBeamColumn3d.cpp`; `SRC/element/dispBeamColumn/DispBeamColumn3d.cpp`; `SRC/analysis/integrator/ExplicitDifference.cpp`). An explicit FemoraX beam therefore cannot copy OpenSees. With zero rotary mass the rotational DOFs have infinite frequency and must either be condensed statically, which is an implicit solve, or receive a lumped rotary inertia (for example ρIL/2 per node). Explicit codes routinely raise rotational inertia artificially so that the axial wave governs ([Cocchetti et al. 2015 preprint](https://re.public.polimi.it/bitstream/11311/884363/6/11311-884363_Perego.pdf)). A recent analysis calls this "rather inaccurate" for kinetic energy when rotation dominates ([Voet et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23816v2)). Rotary scaling cannot push a member below its axial limit L/c. It only removes bending and rotational limits, so it is necessary but not sufficient. The choice is a recorded deviation, verified against analytical beam frequencies. Piles deserve the closest check, since their rotation couples to soil through embedded penalty interfaces.

Element-level fixes leave genuinely short components: link beams, 1 m pile segments, rigid offsets, penalty springs, refined bricks at a foundation edge. These still need LTS.

## The design: stabilized leapfrog LTS, structure fine, soil coarse

### Why stabilization is mandatory, and what it costs

The original leapfrog LTS "may become unstable at certain discrete values of the time step". Its convergence proof holds only under a CFL condition that depends on the smallest elements ([Grote, Mehlin & Sauter, arXiv:1703.07965](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07965)). For ν = 0 the LTS polynomial touches the stability bound at p−1 points of tangency. **Grote, Michel & Sauter's stabilized scheme keeps it strictly inside and proves a CFL condition on the coarse mesh size h_c alone**, with explicitness, second order, the three-term recurrence and energy conservation intact ([Math. Comp. 2021, doi:10.1090/mcom/3650](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3650); [arXiv:2005.13350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13350)). Hochbruck's group developed the same algorithm independently. Per coarse step, with δ = 1 + ν/p², ω = 2T_p'(δ)/T_p(δ) and β_k = T_{k−1}(δ)/T_{k+1}(δ), the scheme computes `w = A Π_c u^n` once. It then runs the Chebyshev three-term recurrence `z_{k+1} = (1+β_k)z_k − β_k z_{k−1} − (Δt/p)²(2p²/ω)β_{k+1/2}(w + A Π_f z_k)` and finishes with `u^{n+1} = −u^{n−1} + 2z_p` ([ar5iv 2005.13350](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2005.13350)). ν = 0 recovers Diaz–Grote and p = 1 recovers leapfrog, so one code path covers all three. The authors restrict ν ≤ 1/2 and use **ν = 0.05**. The sufficient bound `C(Δt/h_c)² ≤ 4e^{−ν}` costs about 2.5 % of the coarse step. The structural-subcycling instabilities above show what happens without such a guarantee.

### The variant to implement

FemoraX should implement the stabilized multilevel LF-LTS with ratio 2 per level and ν = 0.05. It should be written on the displacement three-term recurrence, so that one level is today's CentralDifference / ExplicitDifference bit for bit. Each element goes to the coarsest level whose step is at most 0.9 of its own `critical_dt`, a margin against the discrete-Δt resonances. Each equation takes the finest level of the elements touching it. One structural consequence matters for the kernels. **The fine substeps apply `A Π_f`, so every element touching a fine DOF is evaluated at every substep**: the fine elements plus a ring of coarse neighbours, with the coarse-only contribution held in `w`. The fine batch is therefore the fine set plus its halo, not the fine elements alone (an inference from the formulation; Rietmann's continuous-FE adaptation addresses it, details not verified here). FemoraX's DOF elimination already merges each equalDOF, rigid-link or diaphragm group into one equation. Giving that equation the finest level of any element touching the group keeps every constraint inside one level automatically, which avoids the Abaqus zone-merging and constraint restrictions by construction.

The work-based speedup against a single global step at the structure's step is about r/(1 + f(r−1)) for a fine fraction f and step ratio r. With f ≈ 10⁻³ and r = 2–20 this is essentially r before overheads (arithmetic, not a measured figure).

### What should complement it

Four complements belong alongside LTS. First, the element-level changes above (one force-based element per member, a deliberate lumped rotary inertia, hinge integration for softening members), which cut r before LTS starts and remove the stiffest bending limits. Second, opt-in conventional mass scaling for isolated tiny elements whose ratio would otherwise add a whole level, with mandatory reporting and αM damping on the physical mass. Third, Kokkos::Graph capture of each coarse step's level launches, the direct analogue of SeisSol's CUDA/HIP graph capture, which SeisSol enables by default because its most frequently updated clusters "tend to be extremely small" ([SeisSol GPU docs](https://seissol.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gpus.html); [TUM thesis](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1729783/1729783.pdf)). Fourth, IMEX/HATI coupling, kept on the shelf for components needing ratios of 100 or more. SMS and reciprocal mass are research options, not roadmap items.

### Open research risks

**Damping in LTS is unsolved in the literature.** Published LTS covers only diagonal (mass-type) damping: Grote & Mitkova's LTS-LFCN2 treats it with `(I ± Δt/(2p)·D)` factors, second order, and cannot go beyond order two ([arXiv:1205.0654](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0654)). An Adams–Bashforth LTS reaches higher order ([arXiv:1109.4480](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.4480v2)). αM Rayleigh damping maps onto LFCN2 per level. Nothing was found on stiffness-proportional damping, ExplicitDifference's lagged half-step velocity, or memory-variable filter damping under LF-LTS. SPECFEM3D supports attenuation and LTS, but whether the two run together is not documented ([SPECFEM3D features](https://specfem3d.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features/)). FemoraX should derive and test stability per level in sequence: undamped, then αM, then βK, then Uniform, with each element's filters stepped by its own level's `StepContext`.

**Nonlinear materials break the superposition the scheme relies on.** The LTS splits forces as `A Π_c u + A Π_f z`, which is exact only for linear operators. For a plastic element touching fine DOFs, the natural generalization is to evaluate its full force at each substep with coarse DOFs frozen and commit its state at its own substeps. It is explicit and consistent but has no stability proof. The LFC "nonlinear part by leapfrog" analysis supports it only when the nonlinearity is in the non-stiff part ([CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)). Energy-conserving subcycling loses accuracy with yielding interface material ([Gates, Matouš & Heath 2008](https://www3.nd.edu/~kmatous/Papers/IJNME_AMVI.pdf)), so the level boundary should sit in elastic soil, which is a design rule rather than a published result.

**Constraints and penalty interfaces across levels** have no published LTS analysis. The equation-level rule handles DOF-eliminated constraints. Embedded penalty pile-soil interfaces belong in the fine level together with their attached soil ring.

**GPU launch overhead** grows as 2^L launches per coarse step for the finest level. A fine level of a few thousand elements can cost as much in launch latency as the soil step, so it must be measured before the launch strategy (graphs or fused small levels) is fixed. Rietmann's thesis reports 30x or more from GPU, LTS and scaling optimizations combined, not LTS alone ([USI thesis](https://susi.usi.ch/usi/documents/318539)).

**Multi-GPU load balance** is the documented failure mode: standard partitioning creates strong imbalance under recursive LTS, and SPECFEM needed level-aware graph or hypergraph partitioning ([Rietmann et al., IPDPS 2015](https://sindex.sdl.edu.sa/esploro/outputs/conferenceProceeding/Load-Balanced-Local-Time-Stepping-for-Large-Scale/9943772408331)). FemoraX should weight each element by its substep count in the METIS partition, or balance each level separately. The fine halo must also be exchanged at every substep.

No production structural or geotechnical code was found that runs LF-LTS with plasticity, Rayleigh stiffness damping and multipoint constraints together. FemoraX would be first, which is both the risk and the contribution.

## Conclusion

The question "LTS or mass scaling?" is the wrong framing for SSI. Commercial crash codes default to mass scaling because their quantities of interest tolerate small inertia errors and their step ratios are huge. SSI reverses both conditions: the structure's inertia is the signal, and the ratio is modest (2–20). In that regime an explicit, conservative, physics-preserving multirate scheme beats every inertia or operator modification, and the stabilization published in 2021 removes the instability that kept engineering subcycling niche. The order of work follows from the evidence. First fix the beam (one force-based element per member, a deliberate rotary inertia, hinge integration), because that alone moves most columns into the soil's step range and makes the OpenSees rotary-mass gap explicit. Then build undamped stabilized LTS with levels on equations. Treat damping and plasticity across levels as the research program, with energy and stability tests per term.

What is new in this synthesis is where the true difficulty lies. It is not the integrator, whose theory is settled for linear undamped problems. The difficulty is the boundary between levels: the halo of coarse elements evaluated at fine rates, the nonlinear force that no longer superposes, the damping terms no LTS paper treats, and the GPUs that wait on a tiny fine level. Placing the level boundary in elastic soil, keeping constraint groups whole, and weighting partitions by substep count turn most of those risks into engineering rules. Damping across levels remains the one result FemoraX must derive itself before claiming LTS for production SSI.
