# Stabilized explicit and exponential integrators, multirate methods, and beam-column element remedies for a step limited by a few stiff elements

Scope: (A) leapfrog-Chebyshev (LFC) and stabilized local time stepping, exponential, Gautschi and Krylov integrators, and multirate methods; (B) element-level remedies for beam-columns (force-based elements, one element per member, mass and rotary inertia, localization). The target is FemoraX: Kokkos, matrix-free, FP64, central difference with lumped mass, soil at 2e-4 to 2e-3 s, short displacement-based beam elements at about 1e-4 s.

Research method: web search plus full-text extraction of the Carle-Hochbruck-Sturm preprint and the Scott-Fenves 2006 paper. Facts about OpenSees were checked in the local OpenSees clone (`../OpenSees`, commit `955ceb0a5`). Several publisher PDFs (Gendy & Ayoub, the LS-DYNA and Abaqus theory pages) were blocked, so claims that rest on them are marked.

## A1. Leapfrog-Chebyshev methods: stability region, cost, damping, local use, relation to RKC/ROCK, use in structural or elastodynamic codes

### Takeaway
Leapfrog-Chebyshev schemes replace the leapfrog's `tau^2 L` by a stabilized degree-p Chebyshev polynomial in `tau^2 L`. They allow a step about p times larger at a cost of p products with the stiff linear operator `L` per step, while the "nice" part `g` (possibly nonlinear) is evaluated only once per step. So for a purely linear problem they save little in operator products. They pay off when `L` is the cheap stiff part and `g` is the expensive part, which makes them a multirate or local-time-stepping method. Stabilization (`nu_p > 1`) and a special starting value are essential. The published theory covers undamped `q'' = -Lq - g(q)` with `L` symmetric positive definite and linear. A nonlinear stiff part (yielding fiber beams) and damping terms are not covered by what I could verify.

### Cited Findings
- Carle, Hochbruck & Sturm, "On leapfrog-Chebyshev schemes", SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 58 (2020). It treats `q''(t) = -L q(t) - g(q(t))` with `L` symmetric positive definite "of large norm" and a "nice" function `g`. The linear part uses Chebyshev polynomials and the nonlinearity uses standard leapfrog. — [CRC 1173 preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf); [KIT repository](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000085527/16708333)
- The scheme uses the polynomial `P_p(z) = 2 - 2 T_p(nu_p - z/alpha_p) / T_p(nu_p)`, with `alpha_p = 2 T_p'(nu_p) / T_p(nu_p)`. Here `T_p` is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind and `nu_p >= 1` is a stabilization parameter. The construction is "motivated by the construction of (stabilized) RKC methods". For p = 1 the scheme reduces to standard leapfrog. — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Cost and step size: "this multirate method can be employed with an (approximately) p times larger time step than the LF method … since it requires p times less evaluations of the nonlinearity g". The number of evaluations of the linear part per step equals the polynomial degree p. — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Linear problems: "the p times larger CFL constant is compromised by a p times higher cost per time step (in terms of matrix-vector multiplications with L)". Even so, the stabilized methods with the new starting value beat leapfrog in efficiency, "because of a smaller error constant". — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Earlier Chebyshev-leapfrog methods for linear problems (the paper's refs [7, 14]) "fail to reproduce the long-time behavior of the exact solution for certain time steps". The two remedies are a new starting value that uses `P` and `P'`, and the stabilized polynomial with `nu_p > 1`. Without stabilization, stability of the semilinear scheme with `g(q) = Gq` is only guaranteed under a CFL condition "only slightly weaker than that of the LF scheme", and it "does not improve by increasing the polynomial degree p". — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Results: approximate conservation of a discrete energy over arbitrarily long times for linear problems; second-order error bounds for semilinear problems; fourth order for linear problems with a special choice of stabilization. The paper also discusses generalizations to fully nonlinear equations. "Stabilization is essential to guarantee a favorable CFL condition for the multirate scheme, which is closely related to local time-stepping schemes." In its numerical tests, the more stabilization is needed the larger the stiff dimension; with `nu_p` large enough, the authors observe "an almost p times larger maximum stable time step size and second-order convergence". — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Relation to parabolic stabilized RK: the introduction places LFC next to RKC and the ROCK family (Abdulle, "Fourth order Chebyshev methods with recurrence relation", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 23 (2002), [doi:10.1137/S1064827500379549](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1064827500379549)). Those choose Chebyshev stability polynomials to maximize the real stability interval of first-order (parabolic) problems. LFC applies the same idea to the oscillatory second-order case, where the relevant interval is `tau^2 lambda` on the positive real axis. — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Follow-up: Carle & Hochbruck, "Error analysis of multirate leapfrog-type methods for second-order semilinear ODEs" (SIAM J. Numer. Anal., 2022). It covers equations where "only a few components of the linear part cause stiffness", with step sizes "independent of the stiff part of the equation", and gives a rigorous error and stability analysis of explicit multirate methods based on stabilized LFC polynomials. — [CRC 1173 preprint 2021/26](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2021-26.pdf); [KIT repository](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000133957/117553127)
- Local version: Grote, Michel & Sauter, "Stabilized leapfrog based local time-stepping method for the wave equation", Math. Comp. (2021), [doi:10.1090/mcom/3650](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3650), [arXiv:2005.13350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13350). It modifies the Diaz-Grote (2009) leapfrog LTS following the LF-Chebyshev work and removes the "critical" time-step values at which the original LTS goes unstable. The result is fully explicit, second order, a three-term recurrence, energy conserving, with optimal FE convergence "under a CFL condition where Δt no longer depends on the mesh size inside the locally refined region". The same algorithm "was developed independently and in parallel by the group of M. Hochbruck". — [arXiv:2005.13350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13350); [Math. Comp. record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_crossref_primary_10_1090_mcom_3650)
- The stabilization parameter in the LTS paper must satisfy `nu <= 1/2` (corrected from `nu <= 1` in v4), and `nu = 0.05` is typical in practice. — [arXiv:2005.13350 (v4 comments)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13350)
- Later extension to the inhomogeneous wave equation, analysed with Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind: Grote, Michel & Sauter, [arXiv:2409.18085](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18085) (2024). Earlier convergence analysis of energy-conserving explicit LTS: Grote, Mehlin & Sauter, [arXiv:1703.07965](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.07965).

### Inferences
- The FemoraX "Local time stepping" plan (design.md) is based on Diaz-Grote LF-LTS. The stabilized LF-LTS of Grote-Michel-Sauter (and the LFC multirate scheme of Carle-Hochbruck) is the version to implement, because the unstabilized LTS has resonant step values where it is unstable. Stabilization is a small change: a stabilized Chebyshev polynomial and `nu ~ 0.05`.
- Applied locally, LFC is LTS in polynomial form. The stiff operator `L` would be the beam and structure part (plus soil elements touching it, as in the LTS literature, where the "fine" equations are evaluated p times per coarse step). Each coarse step then costs p applications of the structural operator (cheap: a small fraction of elements) and one soil evaluation.
- The theory requires the stiff part to be linear (`L` fixed and SPD). A short steel column modelled with nonlinear fiber sections is a nonlinear stiff part. LFC as analysed does not cover it. Options:
  - freeze the beam tangent per coarse step (no proof found);
  - use a true LTS with a nonlinear fine-level update (the Diaz-Grote structure evaluates the fine forces at each substep, so nonlinearity is natural there, though the analysed theory is linear);
  - keep the beams elastic, with nonlinearity in zero-length hinges.
- On GPUs, LFC's p fine-operator products per step are p small kernel launches on the structural batch. This is the same launch-overhead concern noted in the FemoraX LTS plan.

### Gaps
- I found no paper applying LFC or stabilized LF-LTS with Rayleigh damping (first-order `C q'` terms) or with recursive-filter (Uniform) damping. Neither the LFC preprint text I extracted nor the LTS abstracts treat damping. A damped LFC needs its own stability analysis.
- I could not extract the exact stability-interval constant (`beta_p^2 ≈ 4p^2` for `nu_p = 1`, shrinking as `nu_p` grows) from the text. The "approximately p times" statement is what is verified.
- I found no application of LFC to structural FE or SSI codes. The published tests are wave-equation or Maxwell-type model problems (not verified in detail).

## A2. Exponential, Gautschi-type, trigonometric and Krylov integrators (and mollified impulse) for stiff wave problems: practical for large nonlinear damped GPU matrix-free FE?

### Takeaway
Gautschi-type and trigonometric integrators (Hochbruck-Lubich 1999; Grimm-Hochbruck; García-Archilla-Sanz-Serna-Skeel mollified impulse) use `cos(tau Omega)` and `sinc` filters of the full stiff operator. Their step-size freedom depends on evaluating those matrix functions (by Lanczos or Krylov) every step, or on a cheap fast/slow splitting. For large nonlinear (plastic) models with Rayleigh damping, the Krylov cost per step means many operator products plus global orthogonalization reductions. The verified literature applies these methods to small or medium elastodynamic and seismic problems only. I found no evidence that they beat explicit central difference with LTS at billion-element scale on GPUs. They are not a practical remedy for FemoraX's case; LTS or LFC is the better-founded route.

### Cited Findings
- Hochbruck & Ostermann, "Exponential integrators", Acta Numerica 19 (2010) 209-286, [doi:10.1017/S0962492910000048](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492910000048). It is the standard review of construction, analysis, implementation (including Krylov evaluation of matrix functions) and applications of exponential integrators for stiff problems. — [KIT full text](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000024914/1910360); [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1017%2FS0962492910000048)
- Within the LFC paper: Gautschi-type methods with exact evaluation of the matrix functions are unconditionally stable. They give the exact solution when `g` is constant and the filter function is chosen suitably. — [CRC preprint 2019/19](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2019-19.pdf)
- Hochbruck & Lubich (1999) Gautschi-type method: KIT hosts the paper ([gautschi.pdf](https://na.math.kit.edu/download/papers/gautschi.pdf)). It cites García-Archilla et al. Not read in full; bibliographic details (Numer. Math. 83 (1999) 403-426) are from memory, not verified here.
- García-Archilla, Sanz-Serna & Skeel, "Long-time-step methods for oscillatory differential equations", SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 20(3) (1998/99) 930-963. The impulse method (Verlet-I / r-RESPA) loses accuracy when the step is near a multiple of a fast mode's period and is unstable near multiples of half that period. The "mollified impulse" method has an error bound independent of the fast frequencies. — [library record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_25821952)
- In molecular dynamics, Verlet-I/r-RESPA is limited by nonlinear instabilities (Ma, Izaguirre & Skeel, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 2003); mollification plus Langevin coupling allows larger steps. — [search record](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/878974605); [Illinois citation page](https://tcbg.illinois.edu/Research/namd/citations/abstracts/WOS:000185337800007.html)
- Pari, Kanungo & Gavini, "Exponential time propagators for elastodynamics" ([arXiv:2405.05213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05213), 2024):
  - Method: a first-order-system Magnus expansion, with the exponential's action computed by an adaptive Krylov subspace.
  - Claim: 10-100x larger steps than Newmark-β for a given accuracy, and about 1000x speedup (linear) and 10-100x (nonlinear).
  - Tests: a cantilever, a nonlinear cantilever, a soft-tissue elastomer and hyperelastic rubber.
  - Not stated in the abstract: problem size, GPU use, and whether the baseline is explicit or implicit. — [arXiv:2405.05213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05213)
- Ravelo, Schreiber & Peixoto, "High-order exponential integration for seismic wave modeling" ([arXiv:2401.16322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16322)) compares Faber-polynomial and Krylov exponential integrators with RK and low-order methods for waves with absorbing boundaries. High-order methods reach "computational efficiency similar to lower-order methods" with considerably larger steps; Krylov converged best among the high-order ones. The abstract claims no cost win over classical schemes. Earlier Faber-polynomial explicit exponential integrator: [arXiv:2211.00084](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00084).

### Inferences
- The Pari et al. 1000x is against Newmark-β at matched accuracy on small models. It does not transfer to FemoraX, where central difference runs at about the CFL limit and accuracy is not the step-setter. FemoraX's problem is a few stiff elements, not a stiff whole.
- Krylov exponential integrators need an Arnoldi or Lanczos basis per step: m operator products plus O(m) global dot-product reductions. With plasticity the operator changes every step, and Rayleigh damping makes the first-order system non-normal. At a billion elements across many GPUs, the reductions alone undercut the "one fused kernel per step, no global sync" advantage of explicit methods. Inference, consistent with FemoraX's own Chebyshev study (Chebyshev cut reductions, not operator products).
- The mollified impulse and Gautschi filters are formally the "fast/slow force splitting" idea. For SSI, splitting "structure = fast, soil = slow" gives exactly the multirate or LTS setting. Without mollification, impulse-type splitting hits resonance instabilities at multiples of half the fast period, the same phenomenon stabilized LF-LTS fixes.

### Gaps
- No source found for exponential or Gautschi integrators applied to nonlinear plastic soil, to SSI, or on GPUs at large scale.
- Grimm & Hochbruck's error analysis of exponential integrators for oscillatory second-order equations (J. Phys. A, about 2006) was not retrieved; the citation is not verified.

## A3. Multirate methods in general (MRI-GARK, multirate RK, multirate leapfrog) and applicability to second-order structural dynamics

### Takeaway
MRI-GARK and multirate GARK are first-order-system multirate Runge-Kutta frameworks. They suit slow and fast partitions with weak fast-to-slow coupling, but they discard the leapfrog's symplectic two-step structure. The relevant second-order families are:
- multirate leapfrog / LTS (Diaz-Grote; stabilized: Grote-Michel-Sauter, Carle-Hochbruck);
- engineering subcycling and multi-time-step coupling (Belytschko and co-workers; Daniel; Gravouil-Combescure and HATI). These have known interface issues: dissipation, drift, and stability conditions on the step ratio.

For explicit central difference with lumped mass, LTS or stabilized LF-LTS is the most directly applicable.

### Cited Findings
- Sandu, "A class of multirate infinitesimal GARK methods", SIAM J. Numer. Anal. (2019) 2300-2327, [doi:10.1137/18M1205492](https://doi.org/10.1137/18M1205492), [arXiv:1808.02759](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02759). It extends Knoth-Wolke hybrid dynamics: the slow part is discretized by an explicit RK method and the fast part is advanced by a modified fast ODE. It gives order conditions and stability analysis, with explicit and implicit methods up to order 4. It is "useful for … widely disparate time scales, where the influence of the fast component on the slow one is weak". Lineage: MIS methods (Wensch, Knoth & Galant, BIT 49, 2009); Günther & Sandu (Numer. Math. 133, 2016) recast MIS as multirate GARK. — [arXiv:1808.02759](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.02759)
- Structural subcycling history:
  - Belytschko and co-workers, explicit-implicit partitioning and subcycling (1970s); Belytschko & Lu (1993), explicit multi-time-step integration.
  - Daniel (1998) studied the stability of subcycling; Daniel (2002) proposed a partial-velocity subcycling.
  - Gravouil & Combescure (IJNME 2001): a multi-time-step explicit-implicit method for nonlinear structural dynamics through dual Schur domain decomposition. It is stable but dissipative, according to Daniel's comparison.
  - The HATI line (Gravouil, Combescure, Brun) extended the approach to large and non-integer step ratios.
  
  Later sources report that GC-type schemes do not keep velocities and accelerations continuous across interfaces ("drifting"). — [search summary and sources: manuscript.isc.ac](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/10/424193.htm); [MaRDI record GC 2001](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:2709654); [CAMES: inconsistency errors of constant-velocity MTS](https://cames.ippt.pan.pl/index.php/cames/article/view/1196); [Karimi & Nakshatrala, arXiv:1305.6355](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.6355v2). Not read in full: treat the details as abstract-level.
- Carle & Hochbruck (2022) frame LFC multirate and LTS as one class of "multirate leapfrog-type methods" with rigorous second-order error bounds. — [CRC preprint 2021/26](https://www.waves.kit.edu/downloads/CRC1173_Preprint_2021-26.pdf)

### Inferences
- MRI-GARK requires a first-order rewrite (`u, v`) and RK stages. That costs more force evaluations per slow step than leapfrog (one per step) and loses the conservation properties that make central difference reliable for long wave simulations. For FemoraX its main value is as theory for slow/fast coupling. It is not the integrator to adopt.
- In SSI the soil-to-structure coupling is not weak (the structure is driven by the soil), so the MRI-GARK sweet spot ("fast influences slow weakly") only partly holds. LF-LTS handles strong coupling by design.

### Gaps
- No source found applying MRI-GARK to structural dynamics with fiber beams or to SSI.

## B1. Force-based beam-columns in explicit dynamics: state determination, one element per member versus several displacement-based elements, mass and rotary inertia

### Takeaway
Force-based (flexibility) elements reproduce the member's nonlinear response with one element per member: the force interpolation is exact in equilibrium, and the integration points sit along the member. In explicit dynamics this removes the short-element limit: a 3 m column in one element instead of five 0.6 m elements raises the axial limit `L/c` by 5x. Bending, which scales roughly as `L^2`, should gain more, but this depends on the rotary mass. The non-iterative state determination (Neuenhofer & Filippou 1997) carries the element residual over to the next step and suits explicit stepping, and an explicit force-based fiber element without element iterations has been published (Gendy & Ayoub 2018). OpenSees' ForceBeamColumn and DispBeamColumn lumped masses have no rotational inertia, so an explicit run needs a rotary-inertia choice: a deliberate lumped `rho I` term, possibly scaled, which is FemoraX's own design decision.

### Cited Findings
- Neuenhofer & Filippou, "Evaluation of nonlinear frame finite-element models", J. Struct. Eng. 123(7) (1997) 958-966, [doi:10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1997)123:7(958)](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(1997)123:7(958)). The paper introduces "a numerical implementation that bypasses the iterative nature of the element state determination" of force-based elements. It accepts residual element deformations and unbalanced section forces during each global iteration; these shrink to tolerance as the global iterations converge. — [ASCE Library](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9445%281997%29123%3A7%28958%29); [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/668339499/Neuenhofer-and-Filippou-1997)
- Spacone, Filippou & Taucer (1996), "Fibre beam-column model for non-linear analysis of R/C frames" (Earthquake Eng. Struct. Dyn. 25(7)), is the foundational mixed or flexibility fiber element. The bibliographic details are from memory and were not re-fetched here. The earlier NSF reports by the same group are at [nehrpsearch PB94117629](https://nehrpsearch.nist.gov/static/files/NSF/PB94117629.pdf) and [PB95192126](https://nehrpsearch.nist.gov/static/files/NSF/PB95192126.pdf).

### Inferences
- Rough numbers: steel `c = sqrt(E/rho) ≈ 5100 m/s`.
  - Axial limit `L/c`: a 0.6 m element gives 1.2e-4 s, which matches the stated 1e-4 s. One 3 m element gives about 5.9e-4 s.
  - Bending, Euler-Bernoulli with lumped mass (general knowledge, not verified from a source): `dt ~ L^2 / (c r)` times a constant of order 1 or less, where `r` is the radius of gyration. A 5x longer element gains up to 25x in bending, so the axial limit then governs.
  
  One force-based element per column thus moves the column's step into the soil's range (2e-4 to 2e-3 s) at the lower end. Very short members (link beams, 1 m piles segments, rigid links) remain a problem.
- With one element per member, the element's highest frequency depends on its rotary inertia. With zero rotational mass (the OpenSees default) the rotational DOFs have infinite frequency. They must either be condensed statically (a quasi-static rotation solve per step, which is implicit) or receive a lumped rotary inertia, for example `rho I L/2` or the Hinton-Rock-Zienkiewicz-type `alpha rho A L^3` choice. Some explicit codes scale it up so that bending never sets the step. This is a deliberate deviation from OpenSees (zero rotary mass), and per CLAUDE.md it needs a `docs/deviations.md` entry. Its effect on low modes (negligible for long members, measurable for stocky ones) should be verified against analytical beam frequencies.
- For explicit stepping, the non-iterative (Neuenhofer-Filippou) state determination fits naturally: each step does one section-level flexibility correction and carries the element residual to the next step. That keeps the per-step kernel cost fixed and branch-free, which suits GPUs. The iterative OpenSees default (up to 10 iterations plus subdivision) gives divergent per-element work and should not be the default in a GPU kernel. Matching OpenSees bit for bit therefore requires the iterative mode; this is a likely `deviations.md` entry or a selectable option.
- The force-based element's stiffness is the inverse of the integrated flexibility. For the stable step, `max_omega_sq` should come from the element's current (or initial elastic, for a bound) 12x12 stiffness and lumped mass. That is a small dense eigenproblem per element, the same pattern as FemoraX's stdBrick exact check.

- I could not verify the exact LS-DYNA (Hughes-Liu, Belytschko-Schwer) bending formulas or their rotary-inertia scaling defaults. The theory manual page renders them as images. Abaqus/Explicit's Timoshenko beam inertia page returned 403.
- I found no source reporting OpenSees explicit runs with force-based elements and their time-step behaviour.

## B2. Limits: strain localization and loss of objectivity in softening members; recommended integration for one element per member

### Takeaway
With softening sections and Gauss-Lobatto integration, a force-based element localizes at one integration point. Its response then depends on the number of integration points, not on a physical length (Coleman & Spacone 2001). The two remedies are:
- Coleman-Spacone constant fracture-energy regularization, which modifies the material law per integration weight;
- Scott-Fenves (2006) plastic-hinge integration (modified Gauss-Radau), which confines nonlinearity to hinges of length `Lp` at the ends and is exact for linear curvature.

For one-element-per-member models of members that can soften (RC columns, degrading steel), Scott & Fenves recommend the plastic-hinge integration. For hardening members, Gauss-Lobatto with 4-6 points (spread of plasticity) remains usual. No single integration rule does both.

### Cited Findings
- Coleman & Spacone, "Localization issues in force-based frame elements", J. Struct. Eng. 127(11) (2001) 1257-1265, [doi:10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(2001)127:11(1257)](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(2001)127:11(1257)). Page numbers are as given by search and were not re-verified.
- Scott & Fenves, "Plastic hinge integration methods for force-based beam-column elements", J. Struct. Eng. 132(2) (2006) 244-252, [doi:10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(2006)132:2(244)](https://doi.org/10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9445(2006)132:2(244)):
  - With strain softening and Gauss-Lobatto integration, "a unique solution does not exist and the computed response depends on the characteristic length implied by the integration weights", which "leads to a loss of objectivity, where the element response will change as a function of Np".
  - Coleman & Spacone "developed a regularization technique that modifies the material stress-strain behavior to maintain a constant energy release after strain-softening initiates". It was applied to Kent-Park concrete with `G_f^c`, and it "requires a modification of the stress-strain properties in the element based on the number of integration points".
  - The new method, derived from Gauss-Radau quadrature, "integrates deformations over specified plastic hinge lengths at the ends", reduces to the exact solution for linear strain-hardening and strain-softening in the hinges, and ensures "objective response at the section, element, and structural levels".
  - "it is not possible for a single element integration method to represent both the spread of plasticity under hardening and have objective member and section response under softening." The force-based element with the new hinge integration "is thus recommended for the nonlinear analysis of frame structures when softening and degradation of the members is expected."

- OpenSees implementation: `beamWithHinges` and the `HingeRadau` family. Modified Gauss-Radau uses two-point Gauss-Radau over `4 Lp` at each end and two-point Gauss in the interior; alternatives are HingeRadauTwo, HingeMidpoint and HingeEndpoint. — [OpenSees wiki: Beam With Hinges](https://opensees.berkeley.edu/wiki/index.php/Beam_With_Hinges_Element); [OpenSees manual](https://opensees.berkeley.edu/OpenSees/manuals/usermanual/628.htm)

### Inferences
- For FemoraX's one-element-per-member strategy, the integration rule is part of the element's `Params` (compile-time `NGP` per rule): Lobatto(Np) and HingeRadau(Lp_i, Lp_j). Both keep fixed-size arrays, which fits the physics-layer rules.
- Softening plus explicit dynamics adds a second concern beyond objectivity: the softening section's negative tangent does not set the stable step (the elastic unloading stiffness does), so `max_omega_sq` should use the initial or elastic section stiffness as a bound. This is the same rule FemoraX already uses for PressureDependMultiYield ("stability tangent").
- Mixed or Hu-Washizu and nonlocal remedies (Addessi & Ciampi, Scott & Hamutçuoğlu "numerically consistent regularization") were requested but not retrieved, and none was verified.

### Gaps
- Addessi & Ciampi (mixed and regularized beam formulations, about 2007) and Scott & Hamutçuoğlu (numerically consistent regularization of force-based elements, IJNME about 2008) were not retrieved; no claims are made about them.
- I found no explicit-dynamics-specific study of localization in force-based elements, for example whether wave propagation along a single element interacts with softening.
